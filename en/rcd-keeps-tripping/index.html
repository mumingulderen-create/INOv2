RCD keeps tripping: how to find the cause

An RCD (residual current device, in Dutch: aardlekschakelaar) switches off as soon as current 'leaks' away, for example through a faulty appliance or moisture. That is exactly its job: it protects you against an electric shock. The trick is to find where the leak is. You can often do that yourself in a few minutes.

Safety first

Never hold an RCD up by hand and never tape it in place. Then your protection no longer works and you risk an electric shock or fire.

How to find the appliance

  1. Unplug everything

    On the circuits behind that RCD (often the kitchen, bathroom or utility room). Also switch off fixed appliances such as a close-in water heater or hob if possible.

  2. Switch the RCD up once

    Does it stay up now? Then the fault is in an appliance. Does it trip even without appliances? Then go to step 4.

  3. Plug the appliances back in one by one

    Wait a moment each time. If the RCD trips with a particular appliance, you have found the culprit. Don't use that appliance again until it has been checked or replaced.

  4. Does it trip without any appliances?

    Then the fault is in the installation itself: moisture in an (outdoor) socket or junction box, a damaged cable or a worn RCD. Don't keep switching it back on, call us. We test each circuit to find where the leak is.

Never work on the fuse box or live wiring yourself. Switching a breaker up or down and unplugging appliances is safe; leave all other repairs to a certified electrician.

The most common culprits

Kettle, oven or dishwasher

Heating elements start to leak after years of use. A classic cause.

Washing machine and dryer

Especially if the RCD trips while washing or spinning.

Outdoor socket or garden lighting

Moisture after rain. Does the RCD mainly trip in wet weather? Then this is often the cause.

An old RCD

RCDs wear out too. Old ones sometimes trip for no reason.
