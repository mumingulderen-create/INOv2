WHEN IS IT NEEDED?

When is an extra circuit required in your home?

According to the Dutch safety standard NEN 1010, a standard 16 amp (230 volt) circuit may be loaded with a maximum of 3,680 watts. To prevent wiring from overheating and fire risk, the following guidelines apply:

1. Large kitchen appliances (> 2,000 watts)

Under NEN 1010, appliances over 2,000 W must be connected to their own, dedicated circuit. Think of a combi oven, steam oven, dishwasher or a boiling water tap (Quooker).

2. Washing machine & tumble dryer separately

A washing machine uses up to 2,500 W while heating water. If the dryer is on the same circuit and both run at the same time, the breaker trips. We install a separate circuit or fit an automatic washer-dryer switch.

3. Air conditioning & heat pump

Air conditioning or a hybrid heat pump draws a peak current when the compressor starts. Its own RCBO prevents faults on the rest of your lighting and appliances.

4. Solar panels & inverter

Solar panels feed power back into the meter cupboard. An inverter must always be connected to its own circuit (often a special PV RCBO) so the total current through the meter cupboard stays safe.

5. EV charger for an electric car

A home EV charger draws maximum power for hours. It must never share a circuit with ordinary sockets in the house and always needs its own circuit with Type A (or Type B/EV) RCD protection.

6. A maximum of 4 circuits per RCD

Under NEN 1010, a maximum of 4 circuits may sit behind one 2-pole RCD. Does your fuse box already have 4 circuits on one RCD? Then we fit a compact RCBO (circuit breaker + RCD in one component).
